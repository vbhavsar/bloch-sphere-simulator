# Script: "A bit vs. a qubit — what's actually different?"

**Release order:** 1 of 15
**Target duration:** ~55 seconds
**Primary visual:** Bloch sphere simulator

---

## Metadata

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Hook | "Your laptop bit is either 0 or 1. A qubit can be… neither." |
| Takeaway | Superposition isn't "both at once" — it's a probability distribution until measured |
| Tease | What superposition actually means (video 2) |
| Simulator scenes | Drag state from north pole to equator |

---

## Script

```
[0:00–0:03] HOOK
[VISUAL: Plain "0" and "1" on screen, then cut to Bloch sphere]
[VO] "Your laptop stores everything as 0 or 1.
     A qubit can be something in between — and
     that changes everything."

[0:03–0:20] CLASSICAL BIT
[VISUAL: Simple toggle — light switch metaphor]
[VO] "A classical bit is a switch. It's either off or on.
     0 or 1. That's it. Every photo, message, and video
     on your phone is just billions of these switches."

[0:20–0:45] QUBIT
[VISUAL: Bloch sphere — vector at north pole labeled |0⟩,
 then south pole labeled |1⟩, then slowly drag to equator]
[VO] "A qubit lives on a sphere. The north pole is 0,
     the south pole is 1 — but the vector can point
     anywhere in between. That in-between state is
     called superposition."

[0:45–0:55] TAKEAWAY + TEASE
[VISUAL: Freeze on equator state]
[VO] "Superposition isn't magic — it's a real physical
     state, like a coin mid-spin. Next: what superposition
     actually means, and why Schrödinger's cat gets it wrong."
```

---

## Production Notes

- The light switch metaphor for the classical bit is intentionally simple — don't linger on it
- The Bloch sphere should be recognizable from the start; this video introduces it but doesn't explain it (that's video 3)
- "Like a coin mid-spin" is the analogy to land — it implies the state is real but unsettled, not randomly assigned
